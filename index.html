Curiosity corner

6 digital scams that catch people off guard

Not legal advice — just patterns worth recognising, since a chunk of "money owed to me" disputes start with one of these.

1

Fake "courier / customs fee" texts

A message claims a parcel is stuck until a small fee is paid through a link. The link harvests card details rather than releasing any package.

2

Impersonation of bank or govt. numbers

Caller ID can be spoofed to show a real bank or tax-office number, pressuring the person into "verifying" an OTP that actually authorises a transfer.

3

Fake job offer / advance fee

A "recruiter" offers a well-paid remote role but asks for a registration, training or equipment fee upfront before any real work begins.

4

Romance / long-distance relationship asks

A relationship built entirely online eventually leads to urgent requests for money — a medical bill, a stuck flight, a customs charge — that never gets repaid.

5

Fake online marketplace sellers

A listing at a suspiciously good price asks for direct bank transfer instead of the platform's built-in payment and buyer protection, then the seller disappears.

6

"Guaranteed return" investment pitches

A contact promises fixed, high returns from crypto or trading and shows a dashboard with rising numbers — withdrawals get blocked once real money is in.

Before you buy

A few things people ask

Do I need a lawyer to use these?

No — the templates are built so you can fill them in yourself. That said, they're general templates, not advice tailored to your case, so for anything high-value or contested, a quick consult with a local lawyer is worth it before you send.

What format do the templates come in?

Every template is delivered as an editable Word (.docx) document, styled and ready to fill in — with instructions and bracketed placeholders you replace with your own facts.

Will this guarantee I get paid?

No. A demand letter is a required first step in most small claims processes and often prompts payment on its own, but it doesn't guarantee an outcome — it puts your claim on record and, if needed, supports the court filing that follows.

Which country's version should I use?

Use the template for the country where you'll be filing the claim — usually where the person who owes you money is based, or where the agreement was made. If you're unsure, that's a good question to bring to a local lawyer.
